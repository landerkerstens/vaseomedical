The system · Radiofrequency ablation

CRF Ablation System

A radiofrequency ablation platform built around the moving-shot technique: a 370 W generator with real-time audiovisual feedback, an automatically synchronised cooling pump, hands-free footswitch control, and a range of internally-cooled, non-expanding electrodes. It treats benign thyroid nodules and uterine fibroids percutaneously or intraoperatively, and the same platform reaches liver, pancreatic and bone lesions — a minimally invasive alternative to surgery in suitable patients. It is the only system Vaseo Medical supplies in Australia.

The system

Four parts, one workflow.

Generator, pump, footswitch and electrode are designed to work as a single unit, so the operator can stay on the patient and the ultrasound screen rather than on the console.

01

RF generator

Up to 370 W

Impedance and temperature changes are heard as well as seen, so tissue response is read without looking away from the ultrasound image. A touch screen carries custom presets, a total energy meter and data capture for the electronic record.

02

Cooled electrodes

Internally cooled, non-expanding

A sharp tip and smooth insulation penetrate skin and tissue without a scalpel. The shaft is steerable, robust and echogenic, with depth marks that show insertion depth and electrode movement during the shot.

03

Cooling pump

Automatic synchronisation

The pump synchronises with the generator on its own and runs regular cooling and temperature checks, removing a step that would otherwise sit between the operator and the procedure.

04

Footswitch

Hands-free control

Fast, intuitive activation and deactivation from the foot, leaving both hands for the probe and the electrode throughout a moving-shot ablation.

Clinical applications

One platform, several indications.

Thyroid and uterine fibroid ablation are covered in full by the manufacturer's brochures below. The same generator and electrode range are used more widely still.

A

Thyroid nodules

Ultrasound-guided, moving-shot ablation of benign thyroid nodules. Real-time audiovisual feedback, automation and electronic records let the operator concentrate on the patient and the ultrasound image while the nodule is treated section by section. The cooled electrode range covers nodules of varying size and depth within the gland.

B

Uterine fibroids

A minimally invasive, outpatient alternative to hysterectomy or myomectomy. Fibroids are located individually under intraoperative ultrasound guidance; a thin, sharp electrode then delivers energy to heat and break the fibroid down from within. Treated fibroids shrink gradually — reported at 50–75% over three to twelve months in the manufacturer's literature — improving symptoms and quality of life.

C

Liver, pancreas and bone

The system's intended use — percutaneous, laparoscopic and intraoperative coagulation and ablation of tissue — extends the same generator and cooled-electrode range to liver and pancreatic lesions and to osteoid osteoma in bone. What changes is the imaging, the approach and the electrode configuration, not the platform. These applications are not covered by the two brochures below; ask us what documentation and regulatory status apply to the one you have in mind.

  1. 01

    Target

    The lesion is located under image guidance — ultrasound at the neck and the uterus, other modalities elsewhere.

  2. 02

    Treat

    A thin, sharp, internally-cooled electrode delivers radiofrequency energy to heat and break the tissue down from within.

  3. 03

    Shrink

    Treated tissue is resorbed gradually over the following months as symptoms improve — 50–75% volume reduction at three to twelve months for uterine fibroids in the manufacturer's literature.

Behind the system

Seventy years of RF development.

Cambridge Interventional was founded by Dr Eric Cosman Jr and builds on three generations of radiofrequency device work — the same lineage that produced the first medical RF generator and the first cooled RF ablation system.

  1. 1952

    Radionics founder Bernard Cosman develops the first RF generator marketed for use in medicine.

  2. 1980

    MIT professor Eric Cosman invents the precision temperature-sensor technology used in most RF electrodes today.

  3. 1995

    The Radionics Cool-tip, the first cooled RF system for percutaneous tissue ablation, is used for the first thyroid treatments.

  4. 2005

    Cosman Medical markets RF devices for neurological ablation in more than 70 countries.

  5. Today

    Cambridge Interventional develops and manufactures the CRF system for thyroid and uterine fibroid ablation.

Indications & regulatory

Before you specify it.

The CRF radiofrequency ablation system is intended for use in percutaneous, laparoscopic and intraoperative coagulation and ablation of tissue. Radiofrequency ablation and coagulation procedures carry reported risks; the brochures above list them and the Instructions for Use are the governing document. That intended use is broad, but individual applications differ in how far they are clinically established and documented: thyroid and uterine fibroid ablation are covered by the brochures above, while liver, pancreatic and bone applications are not. Availability and permitted indications in Australia are subject to inclusion in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) — talk to us about the current status for your intended use.
